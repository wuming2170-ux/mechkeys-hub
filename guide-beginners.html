What is a Mechanical Keyboard?

Unlike membrane keyboards that use a rubber dome to register keystrokes, mechanical keyboards use individual mechanical switches for each key. Each switch contains a spring and metal contacts, giving you distinct tactile feedback and audible clicks.

The benefits of mechanical keyboards include:

  • 🏆 Better typing feel — Distinct tactile feedback helps you know exactly when a key registers
  • 💪 Durability — Most mechanical switches are rated for 50-100 million keystrokes
  • ⚡ Faster actuation — Competitive gamers often prefer mechanical switches for their precise, responsive feel
  • 🎨 Customization — Swap switches, keycaps, and modify your board to your exact preferences

Switch Types Explained

Choosing the right switch is the most important decision when buying a mechanical keyboard. There are three main categories:

🔴 Linear Switches (Red, Black, Yellow)

Linear switches provide a smooth, consistent keystroke from top to bottom with no tactile bump or click. They're the most popular choice for gaming because of their fast, quiet actuation.

Best for: Gamers, typists who prefer smooth keystrokes, quiet environments

Popular options: Gateron Red, Cherry MX Red, NovelKeys Cream

🟤 Tactile Switches (Brown, Clear)

Tactile switches have a subtle bump you feel at the actuation point. They don't click, making them quieter than clicky switches while still providing feedback that a keystroke registered.

Best for: Mixed use (gaming + typing), office environments, tactile feedback lovers

Popular options: Cherry MX Brown, Gateron Brown, Holy Panda

🔵 Clicky Switches (Blue, Green)

Clicky switches provide both tactile feedback AND an audible click. They have a distinct "clack" sound that's satisfying to many users but can be disruptive in shared spaces.

Best for: Home office, enthusiasts who love the click, writers who want auditory feedback

Popular options: Cherry MX Blue, Kailh Box White, Razer Green

Keyboard Layouts: Finding Your Size

Full Size (100%) — The Familiar Choice

Full-size keyboards include the number pad, function row, arrow keys, and all modifier keys. If you use the numpad frequently or want the most complete set of keys, this is for you.

Best for: Office workers, accountants, anyone who relies on the numpad

Tenkeyless (TKL / 80%) — The Balanced Choice

TKL keyboards remove the numpad but keep everything else — function row, arrow keys, and nav cluster. They offer a good balance between functionality and desk space.

Best for: Most users, gamers who want more mouse room, general productivity

75% — Compact Productivity

75% boards pack nearly everything of a TKL into a smaller footprint by stacking some keys vertically. Popular for those who want function keys without the bulk.

Best for: Users who need function keys but want a compact desk setup

65% — Compact Powerhouse

65% keyboards drop the function row but keep the arrow keys and some nav cluster keys. They're extremely popular among gamers who want maximum desk space.

Best for: Gamers, minimalists, users who prioritize desk space

60% — Maximum Compact

60% keyboards have only the alphanumeric keys, some modifiers, and a few nav cluster keys. No function row, no arrow keys by default (accessed via FN layer). The most popular "enthusiast" size.

Best for: Enthusiasts, gamers with limited desk space, those willing to learn FN layers

Key Features to Consider

Hot-Swappable 🔧

Hot-swappable keyboards let you change switches without soldering. This is invaluable for beginners — you can try different switches without committing to a single type. We strongly recommend hot-swappable boards for first-time buyers.

RGB Backlighting 🎨

RGB lighting is purely aesthetic but adds a lot of personality to your setup. Per-key RGB is more customizable than zone RGB. It's not a performance feature, but it does look great and many users love it.

Wireless Connectivity 📡

Wireless keyboards use Bluetooth and/or 2.4GHz dongles. Modern wireless keyboards have nearly zero latency (under 1ms for 2.4GHz), making them viable even for competitive gaming. Benefits: cleaner desk, portability, no cable management.

Connectivity Options 🔌

Many modern keyboards offer tri-mode connectivity: Bluetooth, 2.4GHz wireless, and USB-C wired. This flexibility lets you connect to multiple devices and switch between them easily.

Keycap Materials

ABS (Acrylonitrile Butadiene Styrene): Smooth texture, develops shine over time. Common on budget boards and original keycaps.

PBT (Polybutylene Terephthalate): More textured, resists shine longer, more durable. Preferred by enthusiasts.

For most buyers, stock PBT keycaps are fine — you can always upgrade later.

Budget Recommendations

Under $30 — Entry Level

At this price, expect basic features and ABS keycaps. Great for trying out mechanical keyboards without committing. Look for hot-swappable boards for the best flexibility.

$30-$60 — Sweet Spot

This is where you'll find the best value. Most boards at this price offer hot-swappable PCBs, PBT keycaps, RGB, and solid build quality. The best bang for your buck range.

$60-$100 — Mid-Range

At this tier, expect better materials, gasket mounting structures, wireless tri-mode, and premium features. Many popular enthusiast boards fall here.

$100+ — Premium

Premium boards offer the best build quality, exclusive switches, aluminum frames, and advanced features like OLED screens, programmable knobs, and multi-device connectivity.

Common Questions

Do I need to mod my keyboard?

No! Stock keyboards are perfectly fine for most users. Modding (lubing switches, adding foam, etc.) is optional and purely for enthusiasts who want to fine-tune their typing experience. Start with a quality stock board and mod later if you feel the need.

Are expensive keyboards worth it?

Not necessarily for everyone. Many budget-to-midrange boards (~$40-80) offer excellent typing experiences. Premium boards offer better materials and features, but the quality gap between $60 and $200 boards is often smaller than the gap between $30 and $60 boards.

Do mechanical keyboards make you faster?

Not directly — practice and muscle memory matter most. However, the tactile feedback of mechanical switches can help some users feel more confident in their keystrokes, which may indirectly improve speed and accuracy.

How do I clean my mechanical keyboard?

Regular cleaning involves removing keycaps and dusting between keys with compressed air or a brush. For deep cleaning, you can hand-wash keycaps in warm soapy water. Never submerge the keyboard body in liquid.

Can I use a mechanical keyboard on any device?

Yes — most mechanical keyboards connect via USB-C or Bluetooth and work with Windows, macOS, Linux, and even tablets/phones. Some specialty keys (like Mac-specific function mappings) may differ between platforms.
